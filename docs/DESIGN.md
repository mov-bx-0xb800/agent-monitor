# Interface decisions

Agent Monitor is an editor tool, so it follows the editor rather than a web dashboard: host theme tokens for every colour, the editor's own button, badge and list treatments, flat surfaces instead of nested bordered cards, and no decorative accent bars. Colour carries data only. Category colour appears as a score meter, green marks observed activity, the focus colour marks selection and keyboard focus, and red marks unseen images. No slogan, hero, promotional introduction or repeated product title appears inside the view.

The header has **Main Window** on the left and icon-only setup, pause, help and settings controls on the right. Main Window opens one copy of the monitor as a tab in the main editor area, where the grid widens and images have more room; the control is hidden inside that copy. Every icon-only control has a tooltip and accessible name. Help is a short definition list of the terms used in the views.

There are two views, **Focus** and **Images**, as full-width tabs with icons and a strong underline on the open view. Each starts with a strip of chat cards: provider mark, agent and age on the first line, then the chat title over up to two lines. Focus always shows one chat, starting with the most recent, and lists chats in the collector's recency order with their state and last activity. Images adds All chats, lists only chats that have used an image, ordered by latest image use, and shows that time. Each view keeps its own selection. A provider mark's tile is tinted green by the chat's recency and carries a presence dot while the chat is active. Native horizontal scrolling and previous/next controls, shown only when the strip overflows, support pointer and touch use; Left/Right/Home/End move keyboard focus and Enter/Space select. Selection and scroll position survive updates, and only an explicit selection change reveals a chat. A missing selected chat remains selected with an unavailable message.

Active means an event arrived within two minutes and no end event was received. It is an observed state, not a continuous heartbeat. First seen is the collector's first observation, not execution time. Ages are deliberately coarse (“just now”, “5 min ago”) so an always-open sidebar does not tick every second.

Chat titles are the agent's own where it keeps one locally: Codex's thread name and Claude Code's chat-list title. Cursor chats, and chats whose agent has not titled them yet, use the first useful prompt line. A local rename always wins. Subagents are not listed as chats: their work is folded into their parent chat, so it counts towards that chat's scores, evidence, activity and images. The selected chat's header shows the full title, a rename control, then agent, state, last activity, first seen, subtask, queued-request and image counts, and the folder when chats span more than one.

Focus keeps the category grid, distinct per-category colours and a separate Focus here action per area. Six defaults start in a stable order. Each tile shows the area, its 0–100 score, a meter whose length is the score and whose strength fades with the evidence's age, and the subject count and age. A dash means nothing was observed; a score of 0 with a subject means the evidence has faded. Tiles sit two across when each has room for its name and score, and one across otherwise. Selecting a tile opens its meaning and evidence directly below the tile's row on a shared surface; Escape or Close restores keyboard focus to the tile. Six-dot handles appear in a tile's corner on hover or keyboard focus and support dragging or a keyboard-accessible move menu between Focused areas and More areas. Choose areas saves a chat's visible categories; quiet selected categories remain visible, and More areas holds the rest without reordering them.

Project stakes sit above the areas as a three-part segmented control (Standard, Production, Critical) with a one-line explanation of the selected level; it behaves as a radio group. Stakes are a project property, not a per-chat one. A sensitive change shows as a small warning line on the tiles it concerns, never on every area a file touches, with its kind in the evidence. Custom areas look like built-in tiles with a small “Custom” tag. The last item under More areas is a dashed card, **Create your own Focus Area**: a one-sentence introduction and **Start now**; while the agent works, a note naming it with **Start over**; when the file is found, a title that says what to do (“Ready to approve: …” or “Needs a fix before you can approve it”), one plain line per area (“ready, tested on 6 example files” or “needs a fix: …”), and **Approve**, **Copy fix request** when something needs a fix, **View details**, **Edit file** and **Not now**. More areas then says “1 to approve”. Once approved, the card lists what it tracks with **Create another**, **Edit file** and **Stop tracking**. **Start now** turns the card into a short form with labelled fields (Name; Details and Kind of product marked Optional; Who sets it up as a list of choices, each with one line saying what happens) and **Create Focus Area** and **Cancel**. Problems appear under the field they concern. Inputs use the editor's input, dropdown and validation colours. Wording avoids internal terms such as definition, signals or breadth.

Focus here is a wide, neutral button with a target icon at the foot of each tile. A queued request shows Queued with Cancel beside it and a waiting note. Delivery states stay on the tile. Requests do not fabricate activity, and each request goes to the selected chat.

Images show the thumbnail history first, then one main preview with round, outlined previous/next controls centred on its left and right edges. The controls may overlap the image, have hit areas larger than they look so near misses still land on them, and hide at the ends of the history. Only the picture itself, or its open control, opens the cached copy in an editor tab; the frame around the picture does nothing. Below the preview are the file name, open and dismiss controls, a recent-use line with a green dot, and the agent, age and dimensions. Follow latest is a checkbox and only the checkbox changes it: browsing leaves it on, and while it is on a newly used image replaces the one being viewed. Missing cached files remain dismissible.

The Images tab shows no inventory count. When agents use images that have not yet been seen in Images, a red count appears beside the tab name. Opening Images clears it and outlines those images in red for three seconds. If any belong to a chat outside the current image selection, the selection changes to All chats so they are visible.

A setup warning opens one modal with separate Codex, Claude Code and Cursor states and actions. Each agent that is not receiving activity offers **I don’t use …**, which hides that agent from warnings without changing its hooks. The choice is stored per user and can be undone in setup. When exactly one agent needs setup, the warning offers the same choice directly. Once every agent is receiving activity or marked as not used, the warning does not appear. The dialog keeps keyboard focus inside and restores focus to its opener on close. Installed settings are distinct from observed tool receipt; the warning does not claim to inspect native trust. Pausing shows a Resume message in a fixed position under the tabs. Errors and request notices are dismissible. Setup, untrusted workspace, unsupported remote workspace, missing chat and unreadable cache have distinct recovery messages.

Geometry is deliberately strict. Every surface uses one 12px inner inset and the page uses a 12px gutter, so text, meters, buttons and icon glyphs share the same left and right edges; icon buttons take a negative margin where they meet an edge so their glyphs, not their hover boxes, align. Spacing steps are 4, 8, 12 and 16px: 8px from a section title to its content and 16px between sections. Tiles in a row share row lines, so names, meters, states and buttons stay level even when one name wraps, and every meter reserves room for a three-digit score so all meters end at the same point. Chat cards hang the provider mark in its own column so both text lines share one edge. Controls use editor theme tokens and visible keyboard focus. The grid adapts to one column in narrow views and to more columns in the editor copy.

Motion is short, one-shot and tied to what changed: the tab underline slides, a view or evidence panel lifts in when opened, meters fill when a chat is picked and move only when a score changes, a chat that becomes active pings once, images slide in from the side stepped towards, the unseen count pops when it grows, the red outline fades out, and the setup dialog fades in. Hover and press states ease over 120 ms. Nothing loops, and data refreshes never replay entrance motion. `agentMonitor.motion` turns all of it off, as do the editor's Reduce Motion setting and the operating system's reduced-motion preference. No model calls, new watchers or runtime dependencies are involved. One timer updates age text every five seconds while visible and refreshes attention states every 15 seconds; the three-second image outline uses one short timer. Hidden views stop both, and image elements are reused.

## Validation boundary

Rendered screenshots use synthetic fixtures. Automated checks cover four theme/view combinations, narrow layouts, CSS zoom, keyboard navigation and key interaction states. Automated accessibility results do not establish screen-reader or native-host acceptance. See VALIDATION.md.

References: [VS Code views](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/ux-guidelines/views), [webviews](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/extension-guides/webview), [WCAG reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html) and [target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html).

Icons are bundled Microsoft Codicons SVGs with [attribution](THIRD-PARTY.md), without a font or runtime package.

## Category design

Categories answer “where should this chat give more attention?” They overlap and are not an exhaustive engineering taxonomy. Security and Performance can both apply to the same change. Functionality covers intended behaviour in clients, services, devices and agents. Testing concerns verification; Reliability concerns dependable behaviour through failure. Code quality concerns structure and maintainability.

Do not infer Product from arbitrary research, Documentation from every text file, Analytics from generic operational telemetry, Performance from financial costs, or Privacy & compliance from physical safety alone. Prefer an unclassified event over an unsupported precise label. Give ambiguous words low strength and vetoes rather than leaving them out or trusting them fully. Add positive and negative cases to `test/focus-corpus.js` when changing a signal; the whole corpus must pass.

The category description is shared between the tile and its steering instruction. Keep stable IDs when labels change and preserve the intent of queued requests. A new specialised category needs a distinct, understandable user action; adding more keyword matches alone is insufficient justification.

Design references: [VS Code view guidance](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/ux-guidelines/views), [WAI-ARIA tabs](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/tabs/) and [Google SRE testing guidance](https://sre.google/sre-book/testing-reliability/). These inform decisions; they do not certify the interface or its classification accuracy.

Chats are selected from the strip; local renaming is available from the selected chat’s header. Avoid project/hash labels as chat names. The setup warning deliberately uses bold dark text on a light amber surface, including in dark themes, with system colours restored in forced-colour mode.

Recommendation research is separate from the interface contract: see [research and proposal](RECOMMENDATIONS-RESEARCH.md). No recommendation engine or suggestion UI is implemented.
